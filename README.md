## About

I build software for institutions that still run on paper. The work is operations infrastructure: turning manual, repetitive friction into background systems nobody has to supervise, with the verification trails that let people trust what those systems produce.

At eSerbisyo, we build document services for Philippine barangays, the smallest government units in the country. A resident queues at a counter for a clearance, staff retype the same details into a logbook, and nobody can reliably tell a genuine document from a photocopy. eSerbisyo turns that into a form, an approval trail, and a QR-verifiable PDF that anyone can check. One codebase serves every tenant barangay, and each barangay keeps its own data, branding, and payment account.

The rest of my working life is wider than one company. I have done freelance automation work and infrastructure consulting since 2024, on engagements where the automation either holds up on its own or it does not. I volunteer with BetterGov.PH's BetterLGU initiative, where I built and maintain the community portal for my hometown. And I am a first-year BSIT student, which means the production systems and the homework compete for the same evenings.

None of the stack below came out of a classroom. I skipped a year of senior high school, went up to Grade 12, and graduated with high honors from a humanities strand. The engineering was learned because the work required it, in the order it turned out to be needed. The humanities graduate builds the databases now.

## What I do

**Local government platforms.** Multi-tenant systems for barangays and municipal offices, designed so the fiftieth tenant is as well served as the first. Tenant isolation is enforced at the data layer, not applied as an afterthought.

**Document automation and verification.** Configurable document templates, approval workflows with separation of duties, and issued documents that carry QR codes backed by server-side records, expiring links, and real verification endpoints.

**Workflow automation.** n8n pipelines and self-hosted automation for administrative work: intake, scheduling, reminders, issuance, and reporting, with humans kept in the loop where judgment is actually required.

**Civic technology.** Volunteer-built public information infrastructure for my own city and province, published openly and labelled honestly as community work.

## How I work

- **Specifications before code.** I direct AI tooling the way a tech lead directs a team: precise specifications, written acceptance journeys, and a verification step before anything is called done.
- **Boring release discipline.** Branch, then staging, then production. Exactly one deployment per merge, and automated QA watches the staging gate.
- **Honest scope.** I would rather document what a system cannot do yet than let a pitch promise it. If it runs on paper, I probably have opinions about it.
